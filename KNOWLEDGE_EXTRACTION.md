## MODULE 01: Psychology & Behavioral Science

### Core Theoretical Foundations

**Cialdini's 6 Principles of Persuasion** [Level A]
1. **Reciprocity** — Give value first (free trials, samples, content) to trigger obligation
2. **Commitment & Consistency** — Start with small asks that escalate (progressive commitment); once a visitor takes a micro-action, they're more likely to continue
3. **Social Proof** — 4 patterns: expert, celebrity, user, wisdom of crowds; risks include "empty restaurant effect" (showing low numbers backfires)
4. **Authority** — Credentials, endorsements, media logos, expert associations
5. **Liking** — Visual design, similarity, association with positive things
6. **Scarcity** — Limited time/quantity; works through loss aversion; must be genuine or trust erodes

**Kahneman's Dual-Process Theory** [Level A]
- System 1 (fast, intuitive) vs System 2 (slow, deliberate)
- Most purchasing decisions are System 1 — design for intuition first
- Prospect Theory: losses loom ~2x larger than equivalent gains
- 3 core biases: loss aversion, endowment effect, status quo bias
- Application: frame offers as "avoiding loss" not just "gaining benefit"

**Ariely's Predictable Irrationality** [Level A]
- Anchoring: first number seen influences all subsequent judgments (SSN study)
- Decoy Effect: adding an asymmetrically dominated option shifts preference
- Price anchoring in ecommerce: show original price, then discounted price
- "FREE!" has disproportionate power — zero-price effect

**Thaler's Nudge Theory** [Level A]
- NUDGES framework: iNcentives, Understand mappings, Defaults, Give feedback, Expect error, Structure complex choices
- Default Effect: pre-selected options chosen 2-3x more often
- Choice Overload: too many options → decision paralysis; "jam study" (Iyengar & Lepper)
- Rule of 100: for items under $100, show % discount; over $100, show $ discount

### 18 Cognitive Biases for Ecommerce (3 Tiers)

**Tier 1 — High-Impact (use on every project):**
Anchoring, Social Proof, Scarcity/Urgency, Loss Aversion, Default Effect, Decoy Effect

**Tier 2 — Situational:**
Bandwagon Effect, Authority Bias, Framing Effect, Endowment Effect, Zero-Price Effect, Paradox of Choice, Peak-End Rule, IKEA Effect

**Tier 3 — Advanced:**
Mere Exposure Effect, Commitment/Consistency, Halo Effect, Rhyme-as-Reason

### Ethical Framework
- **Green Zone**: Truthful scarcity, genuine social proof, helpful defaults
- **Yellow Zone**: Urgency timers (must be real), pre-selected upsells (must be easy to remove)
- **Red Zone**: Fake countdown timers, fabricated reviews, hidden costs (dark patterns)

## MODULE 02: UX Research & User Understanding

### NNGroup 6 Ecommerce UX Expectations [Level B]
1. **Convenience** — Easy navigation, search, filtering; reduce steps to purchase
2. **Speed** — Fast page loads, quick checkout; every second of delay costs conversions
3. **Assurance** — Trust signals, security indicators, transparent policies
4. **Precision** — Accurate product information, clear imagery, honest descriptions
5. **Options** — Multiple payment methods, shipping choices, product configurations
6. **Experience** — Delight through micro-interactions, personalization, post-purchase care

### CRE's Diagnosis Methodology (Making Websites Win) [Level A]
**DiPS Framework**: Diagnose → Problem → Solution

**200+ Research Techniques** organized by function:
- **Analytics**: GA4, heatmaps, session recordings, form analytics
- **Voice of Customer**: Live chat logs, surveys (on-page, exit, post-purchase), phone calls, support tickets, search queries
- **User Testing**: Remote/in-person usability tests (most fruitful technique per CRE), 5-second tests, first-click analysis, preference tests
- **Competitive Analysis**: Competitor websites, adjacent industry leaders
- **Internal Knowledge**: Salespeople ("VOC Aggregators"), customer service reps, face-to-face sellers

**Key CRE Insight**: "The best marketers find out — not guess — exactly why their visitors aren't currently converting."

### Gap Analysis Framework (CRE) [Level A]
On-page survey: "What did you come to our website to do?"
Then map: Visitor Intention → % of Visits → Do We Satisfy? → How Do We Monetize? → Revenue Generated
→ Reveals unsatisfied intentions = biggest growth opportunities

### Counter-Objection Framework (CRE) [Level A]
7 reasons visitors don't buy, each with its solution:
1. Don't know what it does → Explain what it does
2. Don't know why they'd need it → Explain the benefits
3. Not convinced it works → Add proof
4. Don't know if compatible → Explain compatibility
5. Think it's too expensive → Justify the price
6. Don't trust the company → Show trustworthiness evidence
7. Going to think about it → Provide reasons to act promptly

**"The problem is like a lock, and the solution is like a key."**
**"For each objection, you need to display a clear counterobjection."**

## MODULE 03: UI Design & Accessibility

### WCAG 2.2 — POUR Principles [Level D]
- **Perceivable**: Text alternatives, captions, adaptable content, distinguishable (contrast ≥4.5:1 normal, ≥3:1 large)
- **Operable**: Keyboard accessible, enough time, no seizures, navigable, input modalities
- **Understandable**: Readable, predictable, input assistance (error identification, labels, suggestions)
- **Robust**: Compatible with current and future assistive technologies

**New in WCAG 2.2**: Focus Not Obscured (2.4.11), Dragging Movements alternatives (2.5.7), Target Size minimum 24×24px (2.5.8), Consistent Help location (3.2.6), Redundant Entry elimination (3.3.7), Accessible Authentication (3.3.8)

### WAI-ARIA APG Patterns for Ecommerce [Level D]
**High Priority**: Carousel/Slideshow (product images), Dialog/Modal (quick view, cart), Tabs (product details), Disclosure (accordions, FAQs), Menu/Menubar (navigation), Combobox (search autocomplete)
**Medium Priority**: Listbox (variant selection), Slider (price range), Spinbutton (quantity), Alert (cart updates, errors), Breadcrumb, Landmark regions

### Design Principles for Conversion
- **Fitts's Law**: Important click targets must be large and close to cursor path
- **Hick's Law**: Reduce choices to speed decisions
- **Visual Hierarchy**: Size, color, contrast, whitespace guide attention to CTAs
- **F-Pattern / Z-Pattern**: Align key content with natural scanning patterns
- **Gestalt Principles**: Proximity, similarity, closure — group related elements

## MODULE 04: Conversion Copywriting

### 5 Persuasive Frameworks [Level C]

1. **PAS** (Problem → Agitate → Solution): Best for pain-point-driven products
2. **AIDA** (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action): Classic funnel copywriting
3. **BAB** (Before → After → Bridge): Best for transformation stories
4. **4Ps** (Promise → Picture → Proof → Push): Best for landing pages
5. **Feature-Benefit-Proof**: Product descriptions — connect features to emotional outcomes with evidence

### Headline Formulas
- Number + Adjective + Noun + Promise: "7 Proven Ways to Double Your Conversion Rate"
- How to + Desired Outcome: "How to Reduce Cart Abandonment by 35%"
- Question headlines: Trigger curiosity gap
- Negative headlines: Work when visitors have already decided to act ("7 Mistakes to Avoid When...")

### CTA Hierarchy [Level C]
- **Primary**: High-contrast, action-oriented ("Add to Cart", "Start Free Trial")
- **Secondary**: Lower visual weight ("Learn More", "Compare Plans")
- **Tertiary**: Text links for navigation
- First-person CTAs outperform second-person ("Start My Free Trial" > "Start Your Free Trial")
- Specificity wins: "Get My Custom Report" > "Submit"

### Microcopy Principles
- Error messages: Explain what happened + how to fix it
- Form labels: Clear, concise, above the field
- Trust microcopy: Near payment ("256-bit encryption", "30-day guarantee")
- Progress indicators: Reduce anxiety in multi-step processes

### CRE Writing Principles (Making Websites Win) [Level A]
- **Memory Buffer**: Reader's short-term memory holds ~15 words; keep sentences short
- **Resolution Points**: Place periods/pauses where brain can "take a breath"
- **Minimize subject-verb distance**: Put the verb close to the subject
- **Omit needless words** (Strunk & White)
- **Replace nominalizations with action verbs**: "carried out an investigation" → "investigated"
- **"Speak first, write later"** technique: Record yourself explaining, transcribe, edit (Moz case study: 52% higher CR, nearly tripled overall)
- **Official Style kills sales**: Write to be understood, not to sound intelligent

## MODULE 05: Landing Page Optimization

### Oli Gardner's 7 Conversion-Centered Design Principles [Level B]
1. **Attention**: Direct focus using contrast, size, whitespace (Attention Ratio = links:goals = 1:1 ideal)
2. **Context**: Message match — headline must match the ad/link that brought them
3. **Clarity**: Clear value proposition within 5 seconds
4. **Congruence**: Every element supports the single conversion goal
5. **Credibility**: Trust signals placed at decision points
6. **Closing**: Strong, clear CTA with urgency/scarcity when genuine
7. **Continuance**: Post-conversion experience maintains engagement

### 5 Landing Page Types [Level C]
1. **Click-Through**: Bridge between ad and product page (SaaS, high-consideration)
2. **Lead Generation**: Capture contact info (B2B, services)
3. **Squeeze Page**: Minimal content, single CTA (email capture)
4. **Sales Page**: Long-form persuasion (high-ticket, complex products)
5. **Thank You / Confirmation**: Post-conversion upsell/engagement opportunity

### Cart Abandonment (Baymard Institute) [Level A]
- **70.22% average cart abandonment rate** (48 studies meta-analysis)
- **$260 billion recoverable** in US/EU through checkout optimization
- Top reasons: extra costs too high (48%), account creation required (26%), too long/complicated (22%), couldn't see total cost (18%), didn't trust site with card (17%)

### Funnel Architecture
- **TOFU** (Top): Awareness → Interest (blog, social, SEO landing pages)
- **MOFU** (Middle): Consideration → Evaluation (comparison pages, case studies, demos)
- **BOFU** (Bottom): Decision → Action (product pages, pricing pages, checkout)
- Each stage needs different content, copy length, and CTA aggressiveness

## MODULE 06: Trust & Credibility

### Stanford Web Credibility Guidelines (Fogg 2003) [Level A]
10 guidelines including: make it easy to verify info, show real organization behind site, highlight expertise, show honest/trustworthy people, make it easy to contact, design looks professional, make site useful, update often, restrain promotional content, avoid errors

### 3-Tier Trust Signal Framework [Level C]
- **Tier 1 (Table Stakes)**: SSL certificate, privacy policy, contact info, professional design
- **Tier 2 (Confidence Builders)**: Customer reviews, security badges, media logos, money-back guarantee
- **Tier 3 (Differentiators)**: Video testimonials, case studies, real-time social proof, industry awards

### CRE on Trust (Making Websites Win) [Level A]
- **"Proof magnets"**: Search for existing proof (reviews, testimonials, PR, awards) that visitors never see — incorporate into most-visited pages
- **"Proof investment"**: Understand what would most persuade visitors, then invest in acquiring that type of proof
- **Company trust ≠ Product trust**: Two separate problems that must both be solved
- **"Conversion is not an afterthought. Conversion is identifying what type of company your visitors would ideally love to do business with... and then becoming that company."**

### Guarantee Framework (CRE) [Level A]
- Guarantee = chainsaw: "Used right, cuts through customer objections. Used wrong, cuts through profits."
- **Two functions**: (1) Reduces risk, (2) Self-evidently promises harm to business if claims aren't honored (= proof)
- One client's sales boosted by almost **50%** just by adding/perfecting a guarantee
- **Effective when**: visitors want what company promises + nervous about risk + service lives up to claims
- **Ingredients**: guarantee benefits (not just satisfaction), address main risks (performance/satisfaction/price-match), offset risk, have a name ("100% Money-Back Guarantee"), word positively as promise, long claim period, easy to invoke, no strings, prompt refund, featured at risk-thinking points
- **9-step safe implementation** including scenario modeling with A/B testing data

## MODULE 07: Ecommerce Shopping Journeys

### Forms UX (Wroblewski + NNGroup) [Level B]
- **Minimize fields**: Every additional field reduces completion rate
- **Single-column layout**: Outperforms multi-column for form completion
- **Top-aligned labels**: Fastest completion time (Matteo Penzo eye-tracking study)
- **Autocomplete attributes**: 30%+ faster checkout when properly implemented
- **Validation on blur**: Immediate feedback per field, not on submit
- **NNGroup 12 Guidelines for Input Controls**: Standard visuals, vertical layout, positive wording, expand click targets via labels (Fitts's Law), checkboxes for multi-select, radio buttons for single-select (since 1984)
- **Progressive disclosure**: Show fields only when relevant

### Information Architecture [Level C]
- **Mega menus**: Best for large catalogs (>50 categories)
- **Breadcrumbs**: Essential for navigation confidence and SEO
- **7±2 categories**: Miller's Law for top-level navigation
- **Faceted navigation**: Price range most used filter; allow multiple filter selections
- **Search UX**: 27-character minimum width, autocomplete after 2-3 characters, show recent searches

### Checkout Optimization [Level B]
- **Guest checkout**: Required — forced account creation is #2 abandonment reason
- **Multi-step checkout recommended**: Each step feels manageable; show progress indicator
- **Order summary**: Persistent, visible at all times during checkout
- **12-14 ideal form elements** (Baymard Institute)
- **Payment UX**: Show all accepted methods early, support digital wallets (Apple Pay, Google Pay)

## MODULE 08: Analytics & Tracking

### GA4 Ecommerce Event Funnel [Level D]
12 events in sequence:
`view_item_list` → `select_item` → `view_item` → `add_to_wishlist` → `add_to_cart` → `view_cart` → `begin_checkout` → `add_shipping_info` → `add_payment_info` → `purchase` → `refund`

**CRO Relevance Mapping**:
- `view_item` → `add_to_cart` = **PDP effectiveness** (key diagnostic metric)
- `begin_checkout` → `purchase` = **checkout friction** indicator
- Items array supports up to 200 elements, 27 custom parameters

### Core Web Vitals [Level D]
- **LCP** (Largest Contentful Paint) ≤ 2.5s — measures loading performance
- **INP** (Interaction to Next Paint) ≤ 200ms — replaced FID in March 2024; measures responsiveness
- **CLS** (Cumulative Layout Shift) ≤ 0.1 — measures visual stability
- Measured at **75th percentile** of field data
- Field data (real users) vs Lab data (synthetic); field is what matters for ranking

### LCP Optimization — 4 Subparts [Level D]
1. **TTFB** (~40% of LCP): Server response time → CDN, edge caching, server optimization
2. **Resource Load Delay** (<10%): Time to discover LCP resource → `<link rel="preload">`, inline critical CSS
3. **Resource Load Duration** (~40%): Time to download → optimize images, use modern formats (WebP/AVIF)
4. **Element Render Delay** (<10%): Time to render after download → reduce render-blocking CSS/JS

**Critical rules**: `fetchpriority="high"` on LCP image, NEVER lazy-load LCP image, preload critical resources

### GTM dataLayer [Level D]
- **5 Critical Rules**: Never overwrite (always push), case sensitivity matters, quote property names, consistent naming conventions, one dataLayer per page
- Ecommerce data layer follows GA4 schema
- Consent integration: load tags only after consent signal

## MODULE 09: CRO Audits & Experimentation

### A/B Testing Statistical Foundations [Level A-B]
- **Significance threshold**: p < 0.05 (5% false positive rate)
- **Statistical power**: ≥ 80% (20% false negative rate)
- **Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE)**: 5-10% typical for ecommerce
- **Sample size**: Determined by baseline CR, MDE, significance, and power

### 5 Statistical Pitfalls [Level A]
1. **Peeking**: Checking results early inflates false positive rate to ~30%
2. **Multiple comparisons**: Testing many variants without correction increases false positives
3. **Simpson's Paradox**: Aggregated data hides segment-level truths
4. **Novelty/Primacy effects**: New designs get temporary boost/dip; wait for stabilization
5. **Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM)**: Uneven traffic split invalidates results

### Experimentation Hierarchy [Level B]
A/B → A/B/n → Multivariate (MVT) → Split URL → Multi-Armed Bandit

### Prioritization Frameworks [Level C]
- **ICE**: Impact × Confidence × Ease (each 1-10)
- **PIE**: Potential × Importance × Ease (each 1-10)
- Both produce ranked hypothesis backlog; ICE is more common

### Testing Maturity Levels [Level B]
1. **Ad Hoc** (1-2 tests/month): Basic A/B testing, learning phase
2. **Structured** (3-4 tests/month): Hypothesis-driven, documented learnings
3. **Optimized** (5-7 tests/month): Multi-channel testing, segments, personalization
4. **Culture** (8+ tests/month): Testing embedded in all decisions, executive buy-in

### Hypothesis Format Template [Level C]
"Because we observed [data/insight], we believe that [change] for [audience] will cause [outcome], measured by [metric]."

### CRE Testing Philosophy (Making Websites Win) [Level A]
- **"GI → GO" (Garbage In → Garbage Out)**: Quality of test hypotheses determines quality of results
- **A/B testing is a stopwatch, not a sprinter**: It measures which version wins, it doesn't create the winning version
- **4 benefits**: Discard missteps, keep only successes, learn direction, never make hard decisions
- **Revenue = Visitors × CR × Lifetime Customer Spend**: Profit is more sensitive to CR than to revenue
- **"9 out of 10 redesign projects go wrong"**: Iterative testing eliminates redesign risk; every new page is ROI-positive from the moment it goes live

## MODULE 10: Measurement & ROI

### 3-Level Metrics Hierarchy [Level B]
- **Macro Metrics**: Purchases, RPV (Revenue Per Visitor), AOV, CLV
- **Micro Metrics**: Add-to-cart rate, checkout initiation rate, checkout completion rate
- **Engagement Metrics**: Bounce rate, scroll depth, CTR, time on page

### Key Formulas [Level B]
- **CR** = Conversions ÷ Visitors × 100
- **RPV** = Revenue ÷ Visitors
- **AOV** = Revenue ÷ Orders
- **Cart Abandonment Rate** = (1 - Completed Purchases ÷ Carts Created) × 100
- **CAC** = Total Acquisition Cost ÷ New Customers
- **ROAS** = Revenue ÷ Ad Spend
- **CLV** = AOV × Purchase Frequency × Customer Lifespan

### Industry CR Benchmarks [Level B]
- Fashion/Apparel: 1.5-2.5%
- Health & Wellness: 2.5-4%
- Overall ecommerce: ~2-3%
- B2B/SaaS: 2-5% (varies widely)

### A/B Test ROI Calculation [Level B]
4-step method:
1. Calculate monthly revenue uplift from winning variant
2. Annualize the uplift
3. Apply **40% degradation discount** (uplift diminishes over time)
4. Compare conservative annual value to test cost

**Example**: $10K test cost → $202.5K conservative annual value = **1,925% ROI**

### 7 High-Value Segmentation Dimensions [Level B]
Device type, Traffic source, New vs returning, Geographic, Customer value tier, Purchase history, Behavior-based (engaged vs passive)

- `POST cart/change.js` — Single line item modification

## SYNTHESIS: Expert Decision Patterns

### The PABLO Decision Framework
When analyzing any ecommerce page, the skill should follow this diagnostic sequence:

1. **DIAGNOSE** (What's wrong?)
   - Identify which of the 7 counter-objections are unaddressed
   - Check the 14 winning website characteristics
   - Review against the 6 UX expectations
   - Analyze the funnel stage and appropriate content depth

2. **PRIORITIZE** (What matters most?)
   - Use ICE/PIE scoring
   - Map objections × traffic = impact
   - Focus on pages with highest traffic AND highest drop-off

3. **PRESCRIBE** (What to do?)
   - Apply relevant cognitive biases from the 3-tier framework
   - Use appropriate copywriting framework for the page type
   - Implement trust signals at the right tier for the audience
   - Ensure accessibility compliance (WCAG 2.2)

4. **IMPLEMENT** (How to build it?)
   - Shopify Liquid patterns for each conversion lever
   - GA4 events for measurement
   - Core Web Vitals optimization
   - A/B test hypothesis and measurement plan

5. **VALIDATE** (Did it work?)
   - Statistical rigor (p<0.05, 80% power, avoid 5 pitfalls)
   - ROI calculation with 40% degradation discount
   - Segment analysis (7 dimensions)
   - Document learnings for compounding improvement

### Evidence-Based Defaults
When the skill lacks specific data about a user's situation, it should recommend:
- **Guest checkout** (always)
- **Single-column forms** with top-aligned labels
- **Price anchoring** with compare_at_price
- **Social proof** near CTAs
- **Trust signals** near payment
- **Mobile-first** design approach
- **Progressive commitment** (small asks → larger asks)
- **Clear guarantee** at risk-thinking points
